1. Problem Definition & Relevance

Modern blended learning relies heavily on recorded academic lectures across higher education. However, learners face substantial barriers when attempting to review lecture content efficiently:

  1. Cognitive Overload: Extracting core factual takeaways from lengthy 45–60 minute spoken lectures is time-intensive and inconsistent.
  2. Lack of Immediate Knowledge Verification: Without immediate self-assessment opportunities, students suffer from illusion-of-competence biases.
  3. Hallucination Risks in Generative AI: Unconstrained commercial LLM summarizers and quiz creators often introduce ungrounded facts, external curriculum drift, or unstated claims that mislead students during revision.

This project solves these issues through a transparent, privacy-preserving pipeline that accepts audio recordings or transcripts, summarizes core concepts using auditable extractive ranking, and creates 5-question multiple-choice cloze quizzes where each answer is provably grounded in its source sentence.

2. Model & Dataset Selection

Core Architecture & Models:

  • Speech-to-Text (ASR): faster-whisper (small model) utilizing CTranslate2 with int8 CPU quantization. Equipped with Voice Activity Detection (VAD) and automatic language identification with probability scoring. Includes defensive PyAV (av) compatibility wrappers to safeguard against keyword deprecations.
  • Extractive Grounded Summarizer: Term frequency-inverse sentence length baseline with an introductory topic prior (+0.15 first-sentence bias), ensuring high-density factual retention without generative drift.
  • Cloze Quiz & Distractor Engine: In-domain candidate keyword extraction filtering non-trivial tokens (length ≥ 5, excluding generic transitionals like because, therefore, however). Distractors are dynamically drawn strictly from the lecture transcript's own vocabulary.
  • Optional GenAI Model: Pretrained Indic foundation model BharatGen Param-1 (bharatgenai/Param-1) via Hugging Face Transformers for optional 4-bullet abstractive comparative note drafts.

Benchmark Dataset:

The project includes a curated 30-case validation benchmark (evaluation_cases.csv) spanning 7 distinct disciplines: Computer Science (algorithms, stacks, databases), Biology (photosynthesis, DNA, circulatory system), Physics (Newton's laws, sound waves), Chemistry (covalent bonds), Civics & Law (Constitution, fundamental rights), Economics (supply, demand, market equilibrium), and Environmental Sciences (monsoon, water cycle).

3. Working End-to-End Application

Implemented as an interactive web service using Streamlit (app.py):

  • Audio Input & Transcription: Allows upload of .wav, .mp3, .m4a, or .flac files. One-click transcription uses local CPU int8 inference and prints detected language and confidence.
  • Transcript Workspace: Allows manual entry or post-editing of speech transcripts.
  • Factual Study Notes: Ranks and outputs concise bullet points highlighting core concepts, with one-click export (lecture_notes.txt).
  • Interactive Cloze Quiz: Generates five multiple-choice questions with randomized option ordering.
  • Automated Grading & Source Audit: Grades learner answers instantly and presents the exact verbatim source sentence for each question to support auditability.

Key Empirical Findings:

  • 92.7% Gold-Term Coverage: Across all 30 benchmark cases, the extractive summary retained nearly all human-annotated core curriculum concepts without generating irrelevant fluff.
  • 100.0% Source-Grounding: Every single generated quiz answer exists verbatim in its displayed source sentence, demonstrating zero false fact generation.

Failure & Error Analysis:

  1. Low-Frequency Detail Omission: Frequency-weighted scoring naturally favors recurring terms. Critical single-mention terms (such as an edge-case theorem or brief formula) can be outranked by repeated introductory ideas.
  2. Cloze Term Selection Ambiguity: Blanks are chosen using alphabetic tokens of length ≥ 5. In sentences with complex descriptive adjectives (e.g., "mechanical wave"), the adjective may occasionally be chosen instead of the primary technical noun.
  3. Distractor Homogeneity on Short Inputs: On short transcripts (< 30 words), the available vocabulary pool is compact, which can result in distractors from different syntactic classes.
  4. Script Segmentation Boundaries: Segmentation relies on formal punctuation (., !, ?, ।). Unpunctuated speech transcripts produce compound sentences rather than discrete factual statements.
  5. Acoustic Error Cascade: Phonetic transcription inaccuracies in noisy audio propagate downstream directly into generated summary notes and quiz blanks.
